# From Research Evidence to Results Prose

Use when starting a paper from a research repository or turning several result
files into a manuscript section. Do not require a repository audit for supplied
prose. Use existing project records and the user's selected evidence boundary.

## Establish What Exists

Locate the relevant manuscript or outline, result files, analysis scripts,
configuration records, and existing bibliography. Treat README claims as claims
to check. Classify each result as executed, supplied summary, planned, or synthetic;
do not infer execution from a file name or a plausible table.

For each paper claim, identify the evidence, setting, analytical unit, and role.
Roles can include description, comparison, mechanism analysis, control,
replication, negative finding, synthesis, or proof. Choose roles appropriate to
the research, not a closed list of ways to demonstrate a performance advantage.

A useful working row is:

`claim | evidence locator | setting/unit | evidence role | supported conclusion`

Keep this in existing notes or a compact task record. It is a reasoning aid, not
a document required before any writing can begin.

## Prepare Comparable Numbers

For multiple runs, use the artifact owner's
`../../paper-figures-tables/references/source-data-and-statistics.md` to derive a
reproducible summary. Check run identity, validity, configurations, aggregation
unit, excluded data and uncertainty before writing a comparison. Preserve actual
failed outcomes; an unfavorable measurement is not an invalid run.

Do not average across incompatible protocols or silently use different run
subsets for text and tables. If designs differ, stratify or state the relevant
difference as part of the comparison. A writing task does not authorize new
training or redesign of a frozen experiment; use available evidence and identify
the exact missing result when it matters.

## Build the Results Argument

Order results by the questions they answer and the dependencies between them.
Put the main finding where it can be understood, followed by the evidence needed
to interpret it. Keep independent findings as parallel results. Report material
trade-offs and exceptions alongside their proper comparison.

Write direct findings with the measured conditions. If the claim is broader than
the evidence, narrow the claim itself. Do not add a routine list of what the
result cannot establish. Mechanistic interpretations retain the uncertainty that
their support warrants; measurement alone does not establish a causal explanation.

If an approved mainline no longer fits the evidence, present the mismatch and a
concrete alternative framing. Preserve preregistered questions, important adverse
comparisons and the identity of exploratory analyses. Do not search for a winning
metric merely to rescue the story or present a post hoc analysis as prespecified.

## Complete the Handoff

Pass the artifact owner the verified summary or source, metric definitions,
conditions, uncertainty, and intended comparison; complete requested figures or
tables in the same task. Use the resulting artifact values in prose. When a
number or conclusion changes, revisit dependent abstract, Results, captions,
Discussion, Conclusion, supplement, and response promises as applicable.

Keep detailed local paths, hashes, and run IDs in the evidence record. Include
scientifically relevant settings, artifact citations, and material synthetic-data
disclosures in the manuscript. Internal record-keeping is not a narrative theme.

Finish with the requested text/artifacts and the relevant checks. Identify
unresolved evidence precisely without turning a useful local draft into a global
readiness judgment.

## Evidence mapping and presentation

Use an experiment-to-claim or RQ map when it clarifies the argument. The mapping
may remain in an outline or be clear from the section order; it does not require
an extra table, answer box or compliance record. An unsupported claim still needs
correction under the evidence rules, regardless of how the result is organized.
