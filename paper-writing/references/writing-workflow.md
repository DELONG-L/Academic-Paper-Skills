# Writing Workflow and Evidence Handoffs

Use for an outline, substantial section drafting, multi-section revision, or a
full paper. Start from the user's supplied work; completed stages need not be
repeated. These are decisions and deliverables, not fixed rounds of approval.

## Establish the Task

Identify the requested output, available source material, source-access boundary,
and any explicit venue or author constraints. Reuse facts already given in the
conversation or current files. Ask only when a missing decision changes the
scientific position, scope, or authorized work.

A missing venue does not prevent a general draft. Missing numerical results
prevent factual result claims, but need not block supported background, methods,
or an outline. Mark the precise gap and continue independent work.

## Work from Evidence to Argument

| Work | Minimum useful input | Result to carry forward | Check before using it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Establish support | Supplied manuscript, notes, data/results, or verified literature | The main claims and their source locations, limitations, and evidence roles | Separate observed findings, interpretations, planned work, and unknown facts |
| Shape the argument | Research question plus available support | Contribution and section roles; show which evidence answers which question | Scope and verbs do not exceed the evidence; flag unanswered RQs |
| Draft | Section purpose, supported claims, required notation/citations | Manuscript prose with appropriate references and visible factual gaps | Do not turn planned experiments, partial validation, or literature claims into completed findings |
| Revise | Current prose and requested improvement or review issue | Revised text with calibrated claims and consistent terminology | Compare source and revision for changed values, causal status, scope, citations, and caveats |
| Verify | Changed manuscript and available source/rendering tools | Relevant factual, citation, cross-reference, and rendering checks | Report what was actually checked; unresolved evidence is not PASS |

The claim-to-source mapping can live in existing notes, source comments, an
inline table, or a compact ledger. Use a separate file when it helps resumed or
collaborative work, or when the user asks for one. File names and ledger formats
are not scientific acceptance criteria. Structured compliance evidence has a
different purpose and follows `paper-policy` when requested.

## Common Starting Points

**An existing passage.** Edit it directly and inspect enough surrounding text to
preserve terminology and references. Do not reconstruct the paper's entire
argument or ask for a venue solely to improve the passage.

**Notes for one section.** Determine the section's job and supporting material.
Draft what is supportable, with local placeholders for absent factual details.
Use section guidance as a content aid rather than a sentence-count template.

**A full draft or outline.** Identify the contribution, the role of each section,
and load-bearing claims. Reuse a sound structure. If a requested claim exceeds
the evidence, narrow its wording or mark the unresolved claim; a different
research direction remains an author decision.

For substantial structural revision, use
`../../paper-review/references/argument-structure.md` to decide paragraph actions
and information destinations. Carry out an authorized edit plan before polishing
the retained text. For repository-to-paper work, use `evidence-to-results.md` to
connect run/configuration records to the written findings.

**An early paper without results.** Distinguish a planned method or evaluation
from completed work. Return a useful supported draft or outline at that stage.
Never fill the results narrative with plausible numbers or present synthetic
values as real findings.

## Handoffs that Preserve Meaning

For a figure/table, pass the claim, data/content locations, units and uncertainty,
comparison boundary, labels, and intended use. Load
`artifact-handoffs.md` and complete requested artifacts through
`paper-figures-tables` within the same task. Inspect the returned artifact and
update the prose to match its actual values and labels.

For critique or reviewer comments, use `paper-review`. The handoff consists of
the current manuscript, requested scope, available evidence, and relevant
comments. A request to draft an author response does not establish that a
promised revision or experiment has been completed. Verify any completion claim
against current files or results.

When a citation gap needs verification, use `citation-integration.md` within the
source permissions already established. A source identifier proves neither that
the source supports the claim nor that an inaccessible paper was read.

## Changes and Completion

When a source changes, revisit its dependent claims, figures/tables, abstract,
conclusion, and response promises. Keep unrelated completed work intact. For a
formal compliance record, the assessor's source snapshots determine whether its
evidence remains current; prose notes are not a substitute.

After substantial revision, use
`../../paper-review/references/revision-closure.md` to decide whether an observed
problem warrants further work. Do not run another full rewrite solely because
style suggestions remain. Complete any remaining required local fixes.

For ordinary writing, completion means delivering the requested supported text
and relevant checks, with material gaps explicit. A user can receive a useful
draft while some claims remain unresolved. Formal submission readiness is a
separate requested judgment and requires the evidence-backed policy workflow.
Do not treat a completed draft, a successful compile, or a style adaptation as
proof of submission readiness.
